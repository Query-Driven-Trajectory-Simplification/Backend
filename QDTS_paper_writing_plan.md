## Executive summary

Write the paper as an analysis paper with a method inside it, but change what it claims. **The evidence supports "QDTS is nearly separable once points are priced against one global budget". It supports "allocation is the largest single lever at aggressive compression". It does not support "it is the budget split, not which points are kept".** On the repo's fixed-order ladder, allocation explains **64% / 52% / 44% / 47%** of the teacher's J4 gain over `RDP_a0` at shares of 0.25 / 0.5 / 1 / 2%. Per-trajectory recipe choice explains **29% / 44% / 55% / 53%**, so it overtakes allocation at 1% and above. Loop plus trades, the cross-trajectory coordination, explains only **6.7% / 3.9% / 1.5% / 0.0%** [Repo, R:L174-183].

The ladder applies allocation first, which tends to favour allocation. A rough single-seed check suggests an order-free split would move recipe choice up, not down (Section C). So "not which points are kept" is contradicted at the two larger budgets. The word "dwarfed" fits only the coordination step. That matches how Leis et al. kept it for the factor that was truly an order of magnitude smaller (Leis et al. [6]).

The headline method result is strong and stable. **`BUDGET_LOOP_TRADE` reaches J4 0.5228 / 0.6837 / 0.8247 / 0.9225 and wins 5/5 splits at every budget.** Its margin over the best non-teacher baseline is **+0.110 / +0.110 / +0.100 / +0.077** [Repo, R:L51-155, R:L176-179]. Even so, the paper is not submittable yet. Ranked by reviewer risk, the blockers are:

1. **No RL4QDTS numbers and only one MLSimp seed.**
2. **GeoLife only**, when every comparable paper uses 3–5 datasets (RL4QDTS [7]; MLSimp [8]; Zhang et al. [9]).
3. **An order-dependent decomposition.** The arm needed for the reverse order was removed.
4. **The final per-seed CSVs are not in the repo.**

The schedule in D runs about 28 weeks to an assumed June 2027 deadline. Phase 1 (weeks 1–8) fixes the reviewer blockers: RL4QDTS/MLSimp, T-Drive, the factorial decomposition and the student pilot. Phase 2 (weeks 9–18) builds the full student, a third dataset and the robustness work. Phase 3 (weeks 19–28) freezes results, writes the paper and packages the artifacts.

**The paper keeps a teacher and a student (revised 25 Nov 2026 at the user's direction).** The paper has three parts. The analysis says what matters. The teacher is both the instrument for that analysis and the label source. The student is the deployable, workload-free method. That gives the analysis a payoff, which was the weak point of a method-plus-analysis paper. The decomposition shows that allocation plus per-trajectory recipe choice (`BUDGET_CHOICE`) captures 93–100% of the teacher's gain [Repo, R:L183]. ## C. Claims-to-evidence matrix: the method claim is ready, the "what matters" claim is not

The ladder was computed in a fixed order. `EQUAL_CHOICE` (recipe choice without allocation) and `BUDGET_LOOP_NO_UPDATE` were removed on 2026-11-22 [Repo, TR:L12-15]. **No order-free attribution can therefore be computed from the final run.** One rough check can be made. On seed 7 alone, the single best greedy recipe at equal budgets (`RECIPE_GREEDY_RDP2`) beats `RDP_a0` by **+0.030 / +0.033 / +0.043 / +0.050** (0.417 / 0.591 / 0.760 / 0.881 vs 0.387 / 0.558 / 0.717 / 0.831 [Repo, ML]). That is a lower bound on "recipe without allocation" and amounts to about 23 / 27 / 42 / 57% of seed 7's total gain.

**[Judgement]** With only the two factors A and R, a Shapley share for recipe averages its effect with and without allocation. Using these figures puts recipe at roughly **26% at 0.25% and roughly 55% at 2%**, with allocation correspondingly about 67% and 45%. **[Judgement] on the framing.** With a student, the paper's structure becomes analysis → teacher → student, and the three parts justify one another. The analysis (C3–C6) explains why a per-trajectory student is enough. The teacher produces the labels nobody else has; MLSimp notes that it lacks "ground truth optimal simplified trajectories" and falls back on self-supervision (MLSimp [8]). The student (C12–C13) is the deployable method. One condition remains: **no headline claim may depend on a comparison that has not been run.** That applies to RL4QDTS and MLSimp in C11, and to the student itself in C12.

The repo's teacher never sees the held-out query pool [Repo, TE:L586-604], so "teacher" is accurate and "oracle" is not. Precedent for this vocabulary:
- Gasse et al. include their expert (strong branching) as a reference row in the main table and measure their headline win against the solver default, not against the expert.
- Paulus et al. normalise scores so that the expert equals 1, and their student beats the expert on one benchmark.
- No paper the notes checked calls its expert an upper bound.
(Gasse et al. [10]; Paulus et al. [11]; Learning by Cheating [26]) [Convention].

So present the teacher as a reference row next to the student, allow R > 1 (it is plausible on held-out join), and reserve "oracle" for a variant optimised on test queries, if one is ever reported.

A second challenge: **"allocation matters" alone is not novel.** RL4QDTS motivates its method with "Issue 1: Uniform compression ratio" and has whole-database ("W") baselines (RL4QDTS [7]). MLSimp's Fig. 12 shows global beating per-trajectory (MLSimp [8]). What is new is **the quantified, order-free split; the separability result; and the finding that naive global allocation (`RDP_GLOBAL`) is worse than equal budgets**. The notes found no DB paper that pairs a separability proposition with a measured coupling gap, or that reports Shapley shares for a method's gain. That gap is the paper's opening.

## E. Wording: claim a ranking, reserve "dwarfed" for coordination

**[Convention]** DB and ML analysis papers state the result as a ranking with a scope guard. Leis et al. write that the cost model "has much less influence on query performance than the cardinality estimates" and that "the influence of cost model errors is dwarfed by cardinality estimation errors". They hedge with "it very much depends on…" and describe their contribution as "quantitative evidence for conventional wisdom" (Leis et al. [6]). Engstrom et al. say the optimisations "are responsible for most of PPO's gain" (Engstrom et al. [29]). Negative results are phrased "we did not find evidence that…" Lucic et al. [33]. Marcus et al. write "if there is a single metric that explains…, we were unable to find it" (Marcus et al. [17]).

**Abstract structure [Judgement], seven sentences.**
1. **Context.** QDTS compresses a whole trajectory database under one point budget so that downstream queries stay accurate.
2. **Gap.** Existing methods (RL4QDTS, MLSimp) bundle three decisions: how many points each trajectory gets, which points it keeps, and how trajectories coordinate. Nobody has measured which of these matters, and no method has ground-truth labels to learn from.
3. **Approach.** A query-aware teacher factorises the three decisions. It serves as the instrument for order-free attribution and as a label source.
4. **Structure.** A proposition: exact separability under per-trajectory-additive objectives, plus a continuous-Fréchet coupling bound.
5. **Findings, with numbers.** Allocation is the largest lever at aggressive compression (about two thirds of the gain at 0.45% kept). Recipe choice matches it at ≥1.2%. Coordination contributes under 7%. The teacher improves J4 by 0.08–0.11 over the best baseline on held-out queries (and over RL4QDTS/MLSimp, once that is run).
6. **Student.** Because the problem nearly separates, a per-trajectory student that never sees the workload recovers [R]% of the teacher's gain at [×] lower cost and transfers to [T-Drive / unseen users]. Fill in once run; if R is low, state it.
7. **Implication.** Learned QDTS should score each trajectory and price it against one global budget. Cross-trajectory interaction modelling buys little except for kNN near-ties.

**Contribution bullets [Judgement].**
- A formal account of when QDTS separates across trajectories: a Lagrangian-exactness proposition and a Fréchet coupling bound, together with a measured coupling gap G showing where real workloads depart from the theory.
- An order-free factorial attribution of the teacher's gain into allocation, point selection and coordination, across budgets, query types, two datasets and two Range semantics.
- A query-aware teacher that beats error-driven baselines, RL4QDTS and MLSimp on held-out queries under both the authors' protocol and a stricter one (20,000 boxes, held-out kNN and join, continuous Fréchet), in 2–4 minutes on 24 cores. It also yields the first per-trajectory budget and recipe labels for QDTS.
- A lightweight, workload-free student, designed from the decomposition, that recovers [R]% of the teacher's gain. Reported as recovered share, speed-up and keep-set agreement, on user-disjoint and cross-dataset splits.
- An open protocol and artifact. The evaluation is about 8–11× larger than prior test databases (16.3M points vs 1.5–2M), and every method's information access is stated.

**Candidate titles.**
1. *Query-Driven Trajectory Simplification Is Nearly Separable, and Therefore Learnable.* The structural result motivates the student; it only works if the student's R is good.
2. *What Matters in Query-Driven Trajectory Simplification? Where the Budget Goes, Then Which Points Stay.* A Leis/Andrychowicz-style title. It keeps the analysis in front and fits whatever result the student gets.
3. *Learning to Spend a Global Point Budget: Teacher-Guided Query-Driven Trajectory Simplification.* The most method-forward option. Use it if the student turns out to be the strongest result.

**[Judgement]** Choose the title after go/no-go 2 in week 3. If the pilot R is high, use title 1. If it is low, use title 2 and report the student as an honest result in Section 10. The researcher's original title, "…Budget Allocation, Not Point Selection", is contradicted by the 1–2% ladder rows and should not be used.

## Conclusion

The repository already holds a clean, well-powered method result. The analysis result the paper wants to lead with is stronger in one direction and weaker in another than the planned framing says. It is stronger because separability is close to a theorem with a measured gap, a pairing the notes found in no DB paper. It is weaker because "not which points are kept" does not hold once more than about 1% of points survive. The best paper therefore inverts the slogan: **QDTS decomposes into per-trajectory decisions priced against one budget. Of those decisions, the budget split leads when compression is extreme, and point selection catches up as budgets grow.** That is a statement learned-QDTS designers can act on: build per-trajectory scorers plus a global pricing step, and skip interaction modelling. The student is the paper's direct test of it, which is why it belongs in the paper. It also survives every plausible outcome of the factorial rerun.

With ICDE 2028 Round 1 as the target, time is no longer the binding constraint; **review risk is**. ICDE gives accept or reject with no revision, and a rejected paper waits a year before it can be resubmitted. Three experiments decide whether the paper is ready: RL4QDTS and MLSimp under both protocols, T-Drive, and the student pilot. All three should be done by the week-8 checkpoint (~20 Jan 2027). Phase 2 then adds the depth (third dataset, design 2, scalability) that ICDE reviewers expect from a paper with no appendix to fall back on.
